Buying my first car came with the usual mix of excitement and mild panic. You wonder what looks
good, what feels safe and what you will still like months later.

After living with the 2026 GWM Haval Jolion Vanta, I have a pretty clear picture of what I love,
what annoys me and what surprised me.

The big takeaway? It is easy to live with: sharp-looking, spacious and comfortable around Sydney.
It is not perfect, though — some of its "helpful" technology can become very unhelpful.

## First impressions: this thing looks serious

### Exterior design

The first thing I noticed was the look.

The Jolion Vanta has a sleek, modern shape, but there is still something mean and muscular about
the front end. It has a futuristic appearance that makes it feel like more than just a practical
little SUV.

The exterior finish is more standard than premium, and I noticed some small imperfections. The
styling definitely makes the stronger impression.

## Interior and practicality: spacious up front, less convincing in the boot

### The cabin

Inside is where the Jolion surprised me most. The dashboard feels futuristic and the cabin is
genuinely spacious, with plenty of room up front and comfortable seats.

There is one quirky issue for taller drivers: if you are over about six feet, the steering wheel
can catch your knees when getting in and out. It is not a deal-breaker, but it is one of those
little things you notice once and then cannot un-notice.

### Rear seats and boot space

The rear seats are usable, although movement and flexibility are limited.

The bigger issue for me is the boot. It is small. For normal errands it is sufficient, but road
trips, camping and lots of gear mean folding the rear seats down.

Storage is decent, with plenty of little spots for small items. The main centre console only has
one cup holder that works properly for a regular-sized cup, though — a weirdly specific complaint,
but somehow memorable.

## Driving experience: comfortable, predictable and not a sports car

Around Sydney, the Jolion is easy and smooth to drive. The steering is a highlight: it has good
weight, feels responsive and the car handles well for its size.

The engine and transmission are where I have a few more reservations. Acceleration can have little
delays here and there, and this is definitely not a sports car. Sport mode helps when I want a more
responsive feel.

The biggest weakness is uphill acceleration. The revs can climb quite high while the gear change
feels delayed, so you sometimes need a heavier foot and a little patience.

Overall, the drivetrain is predictable and clearly aimed at city and road driving rather than
performance.

### City driving

In the city, it is simple to manoeuvre and easy to place on the road.

The front windscreen feels relatively small, so I am more cautious at intersections where parked
cars or traffic block the view.

### Highway driving

On the highway, the Jolion is stable and comfortable. Road noise is a little more noticeable than
it is around town, but nothing excessive.

My bigger issue is the driver-assistance technology. I understand why it is there, and for a new
driver it can be reassuring, but the systems can feel like they are supervising every decision I
make.

Lane keeping, for example, can interrupt when I am changing lanes. For longer highway drives, I
personally prefer having the more intrusive assistance features turned off.

## Technology and features: one of the best and worst parts

The technology is probably the most interesting part of the Jolion Vanta. There is a wide range of
usable tech and plenty of customisation, although the learning curve is real.

The menus can feel complicated at first, but once I understood them, the system was rewarding with
no major glitches.

Apple CarPlay and phone connection are generally easy, although I have had occasional
difficulties.

### Safety and driver assistance

The cameras, parking sensors and surround-view system are excellent, and the alerts give me a lot
of awareness around the car.

But this is also where the Jolion can get annoying.

Emergency lane keeping and driver monitoring can feel overly sensitive. There are times when the
car pulls on the steering wheel or alerts me when I feel I am already handling the situation
safely.

For a learner or inexperienced driver, I can see the value. For an everyday driver, some of it can
feel like having a back-seat driver who never takes a day off.

## Comfort, noise and everyday living

Comfort is another reason I enjoy the Jolion. The seats are comfy, the cabin is quiet and I notice
very little rattling or vibration.

The climate control feels modern and works well, but the way it is controlled is one of my biggest
usability complaints. A lot of the controls are accessed through the main screen, which is not
ideal when you are driving.

I like modern technology. I just do not think every button in a car needs to become a touchscreen.

## Ownership experience: the car was better than the handover

Here is where my experience gets less positive.

My brand-new car had scratches, dents and plastic residue stuck inside the passenger door seal at
pickup.

I tried twice to get the issues resolved. The dents were fixed, but the scratch repair above the
driver's-side door handle was not satisfactory.

The car itself has been reliable and predictable for me, but the dealership experience was not very
good. I like the car; I just expected a better new-car handover. You can

## Final verdict: would I buy the Jolion Vanta again?

For me, yes — with some reservations.

The 2026 GWM Haval Jolion Vanta works because it does everyday driving well. I can get around
Sydney comfortably, it looks good and the cabin gives me loads of technology and customisation.

The weaknesses are real: the boot is small, the driver assistance can be irritating, and some basic
controls are hidden behind screens.

For me, it suits someone who wants a modern first car with plenty of technology, comfort and safety
features, and who is willing to learn how everything works.

Would I buy it again? Yes, I would.

It is not perfect, but it has been a comfortable, good-looking and surprisingly customisable first
car that handles everyday Sydney driving very well.

And honestly, that is what I wanted in the first place.
